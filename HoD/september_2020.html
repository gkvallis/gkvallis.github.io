September 27, 2020

Letter from the HoD
Department of Mathematics


Dear Friends and Colleagues,


It’s been a month since my last letter (which you can see at http://tiny.cc/Vallis/HoD) and a lot has happened since then, although perhaps less has changed. I still hope in these letters to talk about general issues, and about our future as a department, but obviously times are not normal; COVID-19, and how the University is responding to that, is on everyone’s minds. There’s been quite a lot of discussion, and not a little confusion, about teaching requirements so let me start there. I’m not going to get into too many specifics, since these will likely change in the days and weeks ahead, and first a bit of background.


For a long time now it has been the intent of University, and in fact of many universities in the UK and elsewhere, to do blended teaching, meaning that some would be online and some would be in person, or face-to-face (f2f). Typically, most lectures would be online and tutorials and practical sessions would be f2f, where safe. A great deal of preparation has, in fact, gone into making buildings and rooms ‘covid-safe’. The University’s guidelines were that students can expect 2-4 hours of f2f per week, which some departments took to be about 1 hour per week per module. Then last week, as you know, the country moved to covid level-4, and the previously-published University guidelines stated that all but essential teaching would go online. The rub, of course, is what is regarded as essential and here there is considerable disagreement at all levels. In the eyes of the government, universities and schools are essential businesses, and the Department for Education is pressuring universities to continue with f2f teaching, deeming some level of f2f as essential. If teaching is predominantly online (the argument goes) then students will not get the education they deserve and would be justified in requesting a refund of their fees. Also, the societal restrictions (e.g., going to restaurants etc) imposed by the government now are not the same as those imposed when we were at level 4 in the spring, and f2f teaching can, it is argued, be made safer than those social activities. Thus, the University is still requiring departments to provide f2f teaching.


Having said all this many of you do have very genuine concerns, and indeed the government is now encouraging people to work at home where possible. Some people have real health issues, either regarding themselves or their family, and some do not in any case partake of social activities. Some people also feel that, given social-distancing guidelines, it would be pedagogically far better to move a class online, for example in the case of a computer lab where the instructor cannot get close to the student’s screen. On the other hand some people are still quite willing to do f2f tutorials. The students too, seem (from anecdotal evidence) to have divided opinions — some really do want more f2f, some less, and some don’t mind. Parents also are making their opinions known.


Given all the above, our aim is to accommodate as best we can all genuine and reasonable requests from staff to minimize or eliminate their f2f teaching. (Please contact me regarding medical issues, and both the DOEs and me regarding pedagogical issues.) Where possible we will try to arrange tutorials and practical sessions to put students who prefer online together with staff who prefer online, and similarly for f2f, or do similar accommodations. In return we do ask that everyone be prepared to pull their weight in one form or another, especially where changes need to be made and numbers are uneven. Covid cases may well continue to increase, and guidelines may change, so we all have to maintain flexibility in our approach. Our DOEs have a near impossible task in this endeavour, and so I would particularly like to thank Gihan, Pete and Tim, who are doing all the heavy lifting with exceptional good grace. Please also spare a thought for Nicky King, our ADE, who has to navigate the turbulent waters between departments and the fabled ‘centre’, and who does so with aplomb.


A few other matters to end on:
Regarding student intake, after all the dust has settled it seems that our first year class size is very roughly the same as in previous years, and this is more-or-less true across the College. Some other departments, such as Law, have seen a big increase in intake following the problems with A-level grades. Given the nature of those grades, we don’t yet really know whether the quality of the students matches up to our expectations and whether there might be some drop out. Nor is it yet clear how many international students will actually show up; they provide a substantial part of the University’s budget so many eyes are focussed on those numbers.


I suppose the financial news overall can best be described as decent: the University is not going bankrupt, we hope to avoid involuntary redundancies, and students are still coming and getting an education. On the other hand large capital projects are delayed – Project North Park is delayed by at least a year – and all but essential hiring is on hold. In our own department I’m pleased to say that many of you have continued writing grant proposals and publishing papers – well done! In fact, over the summer we wrote more proposals than normal. Still, without diminishing the genuine difficulties that many of you do face, we should realize that the nature of our subject means that we do have it easier than some. Finally, both Laver and Harrison are now covid-secure and anyone (including PGRs and PDRAs) can return to their offices, provided the room-occupancy limits are respected.


We will be having a departmental meeting within the next couple of weeks, at which time we’ll be able to further discuss all of the above matters, and you can also ask questions of the various departmental directors and hopefully the ADE, so please look out for that. On a more social note, some groups have found that social interactions with Mozilla Hubs or Gather can work well, perhaps better than Teams or Zoom, and it’s been suggested that we occasionally have a departmental coffee hour, or even a wine/beer hour, or frequent impromptu ones. If anyone would like to take the lead on organizing that I’d be happy to help get that going, if only by making initial connections among those interested.


As always, feel free to ask questions or make comments by replying to this email but don’t reply to ‘all’ unless you mean to.

Best wishes to everyone!

Geoff

Professor Geoffrey Vallis
Head of Department, Mathematics
University of Exeter
