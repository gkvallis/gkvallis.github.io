August 24, 2020

Letter from the HoD
Department of Mathematics

Dear Friends and Colleagues,

This is my first 'Letter from the HoD', and I hope to make it a recurring feature of my tenure, providing news and updates of whatever may be relevant to you or the department. In this one I'm going to introduce myself and tell you how I see things in a rather general way, without going into specifics. But let me first thank my predecessor, Gavin Shaddick, for ably guiding the department over the past few years. In addition to his HoD duties he introduced a new MSc programme in Applied Data Science and Statistics (ADSS) and obtained a new CDT for PhD students from EPSRC. These will both bring many new students, so well done and thank you Gavin! For myself, I've been in Exeter for about 7 years, having previously been in the US for many years. I'm part of the Geophysical and Astrophysical Fluid Dynamics Group, and I have interests in climate and planetary science. You can find out more at my website, http://tiny.cc/Vallis.

We are in a time of transition. COVID-19 is likely to bring in some permanent changes in the way we do business, although the intent of the University is to eventually return to face-to-face teaching as much as possible. We will also shortly have a new Dean & PVC, Zhongdong Wang, and a new VC, Lisa Roberts. It's great to see two women in these positions, and I look forward even more to the time when that will be commonplace and not even deserving of mention. The government also announced new funding for the mathematical sciences not so long ago (and already that is bringing new students to the department) and I think the government does realize we do need a numerate and scientifically literate workforce. Yet on the other hand we may lose significant EU funding. For mathematics and the mathematical sciences these changes bring both opportunities and challenges that we will have to face both as a department and as individuals. We will have to look outward and respond nimbly to a changing world, at the same time as seeking to influence that world, but more about that in future letters.

We are transitioning within the College from a discipline to a department, so let us function as a department, with shared values and aspirations even if our subfields differ significantly. To this end I hope and trust that all E&R staff will partake in research and teaching, and all E&S staff will partake in teaching and scholarship activities to make our teaching better. We are, after all, a university, from universitas, meaning 'a whole', and neither a research lab nor a teaching college. Having said that, we do have a mixed economy and there will be differences among us; a one-size-fits-all model cannot be our goal. Our various academic groups differ considerably, more so than any other mathematics department I know of in the UK or elsewhere. I will thus be encouraging activities that draw us together -- the mathematics colloquium series, department-wide social events, possible large department-wide grant applications and so on -- even as we remain heterogeneous in our individual and group activities. Mathematics itself and the influence and impacts of mathematics -- whether those be in climate modelling, cryptography, healthcare, or something else -- are what draws us together academically. We can and should build on that to be one of the very leading departments in the country of that sort; note that in the upcoming REF we are contributing very strongly to two units of assessment.

Human diversity is also important for us, and within our own department and College we need to better reflect the diversity of society as a whole. I hope to take an active role in promoting diversity and inclusion, both because it is the right thing to do and because we need to draw on the widest pool of talent that we can. Another human matter is the maintenance of balance, in work and in life itself. Different people have different ways of achieving this, especially in academia, and there is no single rule to follow. Perhaps the one maxim that applies to all is to be true to yourself, cliché though it may be.

Over the next year I will be holding various meetings with different groups -- departmental meetings, academic lead groups, groups of junior staff, etc. -- to develop departmental strategies and goals. I hope that, as far as is practical, everyone can have some input to this, and that everyone feels a part of the department and college. I also hope that all of you will also feel free to come and talk to me individually, about anything at all, and to this end I'll be holding 'Office Hours' for a couple of hours every week. Please do take advantage of this, especially but not only junior staff whom I might not know so well. No reasonable subject is off limits, academic or personal, nor is a definite subject required. All conversations will be treated in confidence. (For various specific matters your academic lead, or the DoR or DoE, will be the person you should talk to.) These meetings will start immediately and to book a slot just email Georgie (Georgina Wood) at her address below.

The students return in just a few weeks and it will become still more challenging for those of us teaching classes this term (and that does include me). A great many academic and professional staff are doing everything possible to make this as safe and as effective as possible, but it won’t be the same no matter what we do, and it will almost certainly take more time than previously. So I really do thank each and every one of you for your efforts.

Finally, please let me know if letters like this are appreciated, or not, and there is anything in particular you would like to hear about, or not. (You can reply to this email, but don’t ‘reply all’ unless you really mean to.)

Geoff

Professor Geoffrey Vallis
Head of Mathematics,
